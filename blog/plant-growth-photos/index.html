How to photograph plant growth from sowing to first leaves

Set the sowing date as Day 1 to see when a seed sprouted and developed its first true leaves. Keep the camera angle steady so the photos are easier to compare.

For plant observations, you can keep the time, temperature, and humidity alongside the day count, and turn off the address.

Decide whether to start at sowing or sprouting

Use the sowing date if you want to know how long it took a seed to emerge. Use the first day you saw a shoot if you mainly want to compare the seedling after it sprouted. Keep that choice consistent within the series.

Take photos at three observable stages

  1. Sowing. Photograph the pot. Add the plant name and variety to your note if you know it.
  2. First shoot. Photograph the shoot when you notice it. You might write “first shoot visible.”
  3. First true leaves. Take a new photo once the first leaves after the seed leaves have opened. Use the day you observe the change for your record.

Make each follow-up easy to compare

Keep the same pot in frame and use the same distance, angle, and light when you can. Put a ruler beside the pot if you want a visible size reference. If you track several plants, check the start date and note before each shot.

In Timestamp Camera on iPhone, choose the day-count template and set the sowing date. The day number goes on the photo when you take it, so it stays visible when you view or share that photo.
